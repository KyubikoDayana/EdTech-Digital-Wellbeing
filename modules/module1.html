MODULE 1: THE FOUNDATIONS OF WELLBEING

This module introduces the key concepts and importance of digital wellbeing in university life.

1.1 Concept and Definition

Digital wellbeing refers to the state in which digital technology supports, rather than interferes with, a person's physical, mental and social wellbeing. This concept does not mean avoiding technology completely; instead, it focuses on using digital tools consciously and intentionally.

According to the Digital Wellbeing Institute (2020), digital wellbeing is the experience of personal wellbeing achieved through the healthy use of technology. Complementing this perspective, it is also defined as the subjective experience of an optimal balance between the benefits and drawbacks of digital connectivity (Büchi, 2024).

1.2 Impact of Excessive Screen Use

Excessive use of digital devices has direct consequences on students' health. Research from the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP, 2026) shows that prolonged screen exposure can significantly affect sleep quality, as blue light interferes with natural melatonin production.

The impact is not only physical. Recent studies confirm that constant notifications and digital multitasking weaken attention control, fragment concentration and significantly increase stress levels in academic environments (Büchi, 2024). Understanding these effects is the first step toward developing healthier digital habits and maintaining a balanced relationship between online and offline activities.
