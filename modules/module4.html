MODULE 4: TECHNOLOGY, MENTAL HEALTH AND BALANCE

This module explores the deep relationship between mental health and how we use digital tools in university life.

4.1 Digital Stress and Information Overload

Digital stress occurs when constant connectivity, notifications, and the uninterrupted flow of information become overwhelming. Today, students often feel an invisible pressure to respond quickly to every message and remain permanently connected.

This digital overload directly interferes with students' ability to concentrate and their academic resilience, making it harder to recover from learning challenges (An et al., 2025).

Recognizing that this constant flow of information has limits is the first step in developing strategies to protect mental wellbeing.

4.2 Achieving Digital Balance

Achieving digital balance means using technology in ways that support our life goals without allowing it to dominate daily activities. This balance involves intentionally scheduling offline time and prioritizing interactions that truly add value.

Positive psychology shows that prioritizing offline experiences, such as face-to-face conversations and interaction with the physical environment, generates more lasting happiness and satisfaction than purely virtual interactions (Cao et al., 2023).

A balanced relationship with technology not only improves emotional wellbeing, but also optimizes cognitive functions, allowing us to be more present in our personal and academic goals.

Minimum requirements for a secure password (Microsoft Support)

  • Length: minimum 12 characters; ideally 14 or more.
  • Character combination: include uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers and symbols.
  • Avoid common words: do not use names, birthdays or dictionary words.
  • Do not reuse previous passwords: each account should have a unique password.
  • Easy to remember but hard to guess: long phrases or creative combinations work best.
