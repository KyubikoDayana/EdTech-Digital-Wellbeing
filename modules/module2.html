MODULE 2: DIGITAL HABITS AND SELF-REGULATION

This module focuses on recognizing personal digital habits and learning strategies to regulate technology use in daily life.

2.1 Understanding Digital Habits

Digital habits are patterns of behavior related to how frequently and in what way individuals use digital devices and platforms. Identifying these personal habits is the first essential step toward improving digital wellbeing.

To better understand this dynamic, authors such as Nir Eyal (2019) suggest distinguishing between two types of actions: 'traction', which refers to intentional behaviors aligned with our values and goals, and 'distraction', which refers to automatic habits that move us away from what we truly want to accomplish. Recognizing these categories allows individuals to regain control over their attention and productivity.

2.2 Self-Regulation in Technology Use

Self-regulation refers to the ability to consciously manage one's behavior, attention, and time when interacting with digital environments. It involves making intentional decisions rather than reacting automatically to digital stimuli.

According to Baumeister and Vohs (2007), self-regulation is a key psychological process that allows individuals to control impulses, delay gratification, and align their actions with long-term goals. In digital contexts, this means resisting constant notifications, reducing multitasking, and maintaining focus on meaningful activities.

Developing self-regulation strategies such as setting screen time limits, taking intentional breaks, and prioritizing tasks can significantly improve productivity, reduce digital stress, and promote a healthier relationship with technology.
