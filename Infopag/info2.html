Technology and Mental Health

Mental health is a fundamental component of overall wellbeing for university students. Today digital technology influences psychological wellbeing in both positive and negative ways. Although excessive use may contribute to stress, recent research shows that technology can be an effective tool to improve mental health when used in a balanced way (Torous et al., 2025).

Although excessive use of devices and social media may increase stress or anxiety, recent research shows that technology can also be used as an effective tool to improve mental health when used consciously and in balance.

Fear of Missing Out (FOMO)

One of the most studied phenomena is the Fear of Missing Out (FOMO). It occurs when people feel constant pressure to stay connected in order not to miss social interactions. FOMO increases anxiety and promotes negative social comparisons, especially among university students who frequently use social media (Gupta & Sharma, 2021).

FOMO can increase anxiety and promote negative social comparisons, especially among university students who frequently use social networks.

Digital Tools for Mental Health

Digital tools designed for psychological wellbeing, such as meditation apps and emotional tracking platforms, help reduce symptoms of anxiety. According to the National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH, 2024), digital technologies can complement traditional therapy by providing accessible self-care resources and guided emotional regulation techniques.

Digital Therapy Applications

One of the most studied examples is the Step-by-Step (SbS) application, based on cognitive behavioral therapy principles. Recent research has shown that this type of tool can significantly reduce symptoms of depression among university students (Hall et al., 2024). These findings confirm that digital interventions improve subjective wellbeing when combined with professional support.

Studies with student populations have reported improvements in depressive symptoms and long-term wellbeing when these digital tools are combined with professional psychological support.

Using Technology in a Healthy Way

Experts recommend setting daily limits and prioritizing evidence-based applications. A short digital detox combined with mindfulness significantly reduces stress and improves sleep quality (Setia et al., 2025). Technology should not replace professional care but function as a complement for developing self-care skills.

Technology should not replace professional care, but it can be an effective complement that helps students develop self-care skills, reduce stress and improve psychological wellbeing.
