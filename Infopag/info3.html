What is Digital Fatigue?

Digital fatigue is a phenomenon that is becoming increasingly common in modern educational environments, especially among students who spend long hours using electronic devices. The increase in virtual academic workload and constant social media use has raised screen exposure, increasing the risk of cognitive burnout (An et al., 2025).

The increase in virtual classes, online meetings, digital assignments and constant social media use has significantly increased daily screen exposure.

Symptoms of Digital Fatigue

Digital fatigue refers to physical and mental exhaustion caused by prolonged exposure to digital environments. Common symptoms include difficulty concentrating, mental exhaustion, irritability and eye strain. Studies indicate that this fatigue can reduce information retention in students by 20% to 30% (An et al., 2025).

Common symptoms include difficulty concentrating, mental exhaustion, irritability, eye strain, headaches and reduced academic performance.

Zoom Fatigue

One of the most studied concepts is Zoom fatigue. Researcher Jeremy Bailenson (2021) from Stanford University identified factors such as constant exposure to close-up faces, prolonged visual attention and lack of physical mobility as the main causes of this exhaustion.

Research by Stanford professor Jeremy Bailenson identified factors such as constant exposure to faces on screen, prolonged visual attention and lack of breaks as key causes.

Digital Overload and Multitasking

Continuous exposure to digital stimuli affects learning processes. Digital multitasking, such as attending classes while checking messages, increases cognitive load and contributes to mental exhaustion, affecting students' academic performance.

Conclusion

Preventing digital fatigue is essential for long-term academic success. Adopting healthy habits, establishing screen-free boundaries, and protecting your focus will preserve your visual health and cognitive performance.
